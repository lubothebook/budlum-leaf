# BUDLUM LEAF · THE DIRECTIVE · PART 7 · THE CLEAR FAMILY, THE FRAME AND THE FIELD

---

## 7.1 The requirement law

1. **The user sees the world with the user's own eyes**. A Budlum Leaf adds light; it does not replace the world with a camera image. `[DECISION]`
2. A video pass-through view is refused as a base mode. A camera copy adds delay and limits resolution, colour and dynamic range.
3. **The see-through layer never blocks the world:** the transmission of the clear part is at least 45 percent. `[DECISION]`
4. **(the answer of Ayaz, question 4)** The see-through capability is **not a property of the glasses**. It is a property of the whole family, and every member can use it.

## 7.4 The daylight proof, and the honest limit

```
L_eye = L_world × T + L_display
```

| The scene | The world at the eye through a 60 percent panel | The display must add | The verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| an office, 300 cd/m² | 180 cd/m² | 180 to 400 cd/m² | easy |
| a cloudy street, 2 000 cd/m² | 1 200 cd/m² | 1 200 to 2 500 cd/m² | at the limit of the clear panel |
| a sunlit street, 10 000 cd/m² | 6 000 cd/m² | 6 000 to 15 000 cd/m² | not reachable without the dimmer |
| a sunlit street, the dimmer at 25 percent | 2 500 cd/m² | 2 500 to 5 000 cd/m² | reachable for a text class overlay |

**The conclusion, written as a claim rule**. Without a dimmer, a clear panel is an indoor and cloudy-day part. With the dimmer, it carries a text-class overlay in direct sun. A full-colour image in direct sun requires the waveguide variant, and that limit is written into the record. `[DECISION]`

## 7.6 The Field mode

### 7.6.1 What it is

A Leaf 160 or a Leaf 160² has two faces. A lens spacer with an interpupillary adjuster holds the device at the eyes, and each face serves one eye. The surface becomes a wide, bright stereo window of 85 to 100 degrees. `[DECISION]`

| The property | The value |
|---|---|
| The spacer | 28 to 45 g |
| The field of view | 85 to 100 degrees diagonal |
| The density | about 20 to 25 ppd per eye on a Leaf 160: enough for a scene, low for fine text (Part 15, H-1) |
| The latency target | below 45 ms of motion to photon through the session, the link and the panel |
| The thickness effect | **none**: the leaf stays 4.00 mm, the cassette is unchanged, and the spacer is an accessory |
| The isolation rule | one action enters, one action leaves, a program cannot place the user inside, and the world is one gesture away |

### 7.6.2 The angle table, and why a user wants it

| What the user looks at | The width | The distance | The angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| a Leaf 160, held in the hand | 160 mm | 300 mm | 30° |
| the Field mode | 160 mm per eye at the eye | 20 mm | 85 to 100° |
| a Leaf 1400 | 1 400 mm | 2 500 mm | 31° |
| a 100 inch screen | 2 214 mm | 2 500 mm | 48° |
| the Field angle, on a wall | 7 150 mm | 3 000 mm | 100°, which is a 320 inch image at three metres |

**The sentence that sells it**. The room surface spans 31 degrees at home, and the carried surface spans 30 degrees in the hand. The Field mode spans 100 degrees and fits the same pocket. `[DECISION]` (the answer of Ayaz, question 25: a cinema mode.)

### 7.6.3 The four reasons a person uses it

1. The size that no room allows: a hotel room, a seat, a bed, a small flat.
2. A private and a silent screen: one person watches, nobody else hears or sees.
3. The depth a flat surface cannot show: a stereo photograph, a stereo video, a model.
4. The same device, session and wallet: a film moves into Field mode without copying.

**What it is not**. Not a second world, not an avatar, not a room that replaces the room. There is no account to open and no wall to build.

### 7.6.4 The relation to the clear family

A clear face in the Field mode is refused by design. In Field mode the user chose the film, and a transparent face would show the room behind it. A clear face is the world plus a note; the Field mode is the note alone. `[DECISION]`
