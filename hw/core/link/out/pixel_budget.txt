check against the Lead: 240 Hz raw 10 bit = 36.86 Gb/s; at 3:1, 4:1, 6:1 = 12.29, 9.22, 6.14 Gb/s
  honest ratios of 10 bit input: DSC 10 bpp = 3.00:1 (12.29), DSC 8 bpp = 3.75:1 (9.83), VDC-M 6 bpp = 5.00:1 (7.37).
  4:1 of 10 bit is 7.5 bpp. It sits under the 8 bpp at which DSC is rated. 6:1 is 5 bpp. It sits under
  the 6 bpp at which VDC-M is rated. [PUBLICATION] VESA DSC 1.2b (3:1, 8 bpp), VDC-M 1.2 (5:1, 6 bpp from 30 b).
  So the Lead's 4:1 is at the edge and 6:1 is outside the published visually lossless range.

  Warp in the Core: the stream is 5.12 Mpx at every stretch. The Core sends warped tile images and needs
  the tile angles back at 480 Hz (about 1 Mb/s, Section D4). Warp in the Skin: 1.58 x and 2.00 x pixels,
  a 251 x 251 or 283 x 283 source window per hub, a 4-tap engine and about 0.5 to 1 Mb of line buffer per hub.
  [DECISION] of this study: the warp stays in the Core. The cost is the age of the angles (Section D4).
  The more-room canvas (2007 x 4014 at 25 %, 2263 x 4525 at 41 %, up to 30.7 MB raw) lives in the Core store,
  not on the link.

A5. the hinge mesh forces a decoder on the Skin, whatever the tether carries
  one hinge: 3 lanes x 3.2 Gb/s = 9.31 Gb/s payload. The root of a degree-3 seat
  carries (127/128)/3 of the stream; with one root hinge broken (degree 2) it carries (127/128)/2.
  raw 10 bit   240 Hz: stream  39.08 Gb/s, root hinge 12.92 (3 hinges) / 19.39 (2 hinges) vs 9.31  -> FAILS
  DSC 10 bpp   240 Hz: stream  13.03 Gb/s, root hinge  4.31 (3 hinges) /  6.46 (2 hinges) vs 9.31  -> fits
  DSC 8 bpp    240 Hz: stream  10.42 Gb/s, root hinge  3.45 (3 hinges) /  5.17 (2 hinges) vs 9.31  -> fits
  VDC-M 6 bpp  240 Hz: stream   7.82 Gb/s, root hinge  2.58 (3 hinges) /  3.88 (2 hinges) vs 9.31  -> fits
  minimum compression ratio of 10 bit, 240 Hz: 1.39:1 with all root hinges, 2.08:1 with one broken.
  So a Skin with no decoder is not possible at 240 Hz through this mesh. The decoder floor is set by the hinge
  copper, not by the tether. [HYPOTHESIS]
