B2. copper flex and twinax loss at 5 GHz (the Nyquist of a 10 Gb/s lane). Budget for tether and connectors: 12 dB [HYPOTHESIS]
    USB 3.2 Gen 2 allows about 23 dB host to device at 5 GHz in total [PUBLICATION] USB-IF; 12 dB is left for the cable.
  LCP flex trace, w 100 um, tan d 0.004:  49.4 dB/m at 5 GHz -> 12 dB reach  0.24 m
  LCP flex trace, w 200 um, tan d 0.004:  26.3 dB/m at 5 GHz -> 12 dB reach  0.46 m
  LCP flex trace, w 400 um, tan d 0.004:  14.7 dB/m at 5 GHz -> 12 dB reach  0.82 m
  twinax 30 AWG, shielded, proximity x2:   5.3 dB/m at 5 GHz -> 12 dB reach  2.26 m
  twinax 34 AWG, shielded, proximity x2:   7.7 dB/m at 5 GHz -> 12 dB reach  1.56 m
  twinax 40 AWG, shielded, proximity x2:  14.1 dB/m at 5 GHz -> 12 dB reach  0.85 m
  verdict: thin flex carries 10 Gb/s over 0.25 to 0.8 m, round twinax over 1 to 3 m. That is C0. At 5 to 30 m only fibre or an active cable works.

B3. inductive power through a contactless seat: eta = k2 Q1 Q2 / (1 + sqrt(1 + k2 Q1 Q2))^2
    Skin share of the loss: half of the coil loss plus the rectifier and regulator at 5 % of the output.
    k    Q    eta |  loss at 1.8 W out  Skin heat |  loss at 3.6 W out  Skin heat
 0.40   30   0.85 |            0.33 W     0.25 W |            0.65 W     0.51 W
 0.20   30   0.72 |            0.71 W     0.44 W |            1.42 W     0.89 W
 0.20   15   0.52 |            1.66 W     0.92 W |            3.33 W     1.84 W
 0.10   15   0.29 |            4.48 W     2.33 W |            8.97 W     4.66 W
    A coil pair at k 0.2 and Q 30 wastes 0.7 W for 1.8 W. The Skin keeps 0.4 W of heat. Power rides on contact leaves.

B4. near-field couplers: 6 Gb/s and 6 pJ/b per coupler pair, 6 mm2, at 5 mm (ISSCC 2015)
  couplers for the design case: 3 pairs x 6 mm2 = 18 mm2 on the Skin edge; Skin share of the energy 50 %: 39 mW
  Gap 1 to 5 mm. A kinematic seat of the Part 21 quadrant locator repeats to under 1 um [GENERAL], so alignment is not the limit.
  Magnets sit near the couplers. A transmission-line coupler has no ferrite, so it does not saturate. A magnetic (inductive) coupler would.
