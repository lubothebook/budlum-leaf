## 2 · Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, the battery regulation

### 2.1 What the text says

| The provision | The text read | The tag |
|---|---|---|
| Article 3(1), battery | "any device delivering electrical energy generated by direct conversion of chemical energy", of one or more cells, modules or packs | `[PUBLICATION]` lawplayer.com/eu/act/32023R1542 |
| Article 3(4), battery cell | "the basic functional unit in a battery", with electrodes, electrolyte, a container and terminals | `[PUBLICATION]` same page |
| Article 3(9), portable battery | a battery that is sealed, weighs 5 kg or less, and is not built for industrial use or for a vehicle | `[PUBLICATION]` same page |
| Article 11(1) | the maker ensures portable batteries are "readily removable and replaceable by the end-user" for the life of the product | `[PUBLICATION]` same page |
| Article 11(3) | no duty where continuity of power is needed and a permanent connection is required for safety. Also for data integrity, if the main function is data collection | `[PUBLICATION]` same page, paraphrased |
| Article 96 | Article 11 applies from `18 February 2027` | `[PUBLICATION]` lawplayer.com/eu/article/32023R1542/art_96 |
| the industry guidance of September 2024 | a battery "shall at least be able to deliver energy" generated by direct conversion of chemical energy | `[PUBLICATION]` rechargebatteries.org, part read |

The guidance text the team read did not mention capacitors. **The capacitor answer below is the team's reading of the definition.**

### 2.2 The three tiers against the text

| The tier | Is it a battery? | Does Article 11 apply? | The tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bare**, a capacitor store of seconds | no, if the store keeps charge in an electric field and no chemical reaction delivers the energy | no, if the whole body holds no cell, including a clock cell | `[HYPOTHESIS]` |
| **Buffer**, a few of the `128` cells, `0.3` to `1.5 Wh` | yes. Each cell fits the cell definition | yes. The duty falls on the removable unit | `[HYPOTHESIS]` |
| **Full**, all `128` cells | yes | yes, answered by the removable quadrant of `hw/energy/06` | `[TREE]` |

### 2.3 The hard findings for the Bare tier

1. **The word "capacitor" is not enough.** A lithium-ion capacitor stores energy partly by a chemical reaction. A lawyer can read it as a cell.
2. **A pure double layer capacitor or a ceramic store is the safe choice.** The Bare record names the store type. `[DECISION]` proposed.
3. **A clock cell breaks the exemption.** A coin cell for a real time clock is a portable battery. The Bare body holds none, or it takes the removable route.
4. **The legal gain of the Bare tier is real and narrow.** It removes Article 11 and the endurance duty. Any cell that stays removes the gain.

### 2.4 The Buffer tier

| The question | The answer |
|---|---|
| Can the Buffer be a few loose cells fixed in the body? | no. That fails Article 11 unless an exemption applies. `[HYPOTHESIS]` |
| Can the Buffer be one quadrant? | yes. One quadrant holds `32` cells and is the removable unit of `hw/energy/06`. It is `1.476 Wh` in variant G. `[TREE]` |
| Does Article 11(3) save a fixed Buffer? | not safely. The Leaf main function is a surface and a wallet, not data collection. `FINAL/17` Section 17.7 already calls the argument unsettled. `[HYPOTHESIS]` |
| Does a bridge that keeps a wallet session count as safety? | the team found no text that says so. A lawyer decides. `[HYPOTHESIS]` |

**The recommendation.** Build the Buffer tier as one fitted quadrant. It reuses the answer the program already chose. `[DECISION]` proposed.

## 5 · The host classes H0 to H6

The margin is `P_avail x efficiency - 8.7 W`. A negative margin needs the Buffer tier to carry the peak. The efficiencies are assumptions. `[HYPOTHESIS]`

| Class | The host | The power read | The margin against `8.7 W` | The standard that binds | The tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H0 | wall dock, `40 W` USB-C | `40 W` | `+27.3 W` | the dock and its adapter carry the mains safety. Output is ES1 | `[TREE]` `[HYPOTHESIS]` |
| H1 | dock pack, `20 Wh` | an energy of `20 Wh` | `20 Wh / 8.7 W` is about `2.3 h` at the peak | the pack is a portable battery. Article 11 gives it a door with two screws | `[TREE]` |

| H4 | industrial and PoE | class 3 gives `13 W` | `+2.4 W`. Class 1 and 2 fail at `-5.4` and `-3.2 W` | IEEE 802.3 and the isolation of the port | `[PUBLICATION]` Ethernet Alliance |
| H5 | worn power bank | the bank decides | none until the bank is named | the bank is a separate product with its own CE and cell approval. The Leaf sees USB-C | `[HYPOTHESIS]` |
| H6 | low power Qi pad | `5 W` basic, `15 W` or `25 W` after authentication | `-5.0 W`, `+2.6 W`, `+10.1 W` | Qi authentication and certification. Heat `5.8` to `6.9 W` at `25 W` | `[PUBLICATION]` GRL, WPC |

### 5.1 The five findings that decide the host classes

1. **A host that cannot carry the peak can carry the mean.** The session mean is `1.6` to `3.7 W`. Only H2 on a `2.5 W` port and H4 class 1 fail the top.
2. **The peak is the problem, not the hour.** Samsung cuts speed and brightness in No Battery Mode. `[PUBLICATION]` The Leaf shapes the peak or uses the Buffer.
3. **The Bare tier lives on H0, H2 with PD, PoE class 3 and up, Qi2 from `15 W`.**
   Other hosts fail at the peak. A shaped peak is a powerpath test.
4. **Qi at `25 W` fails the heat law first.**
   Its `5.8` to `6.9 W` of body heat dwarfs the contact figure of `0.1` to `0.34 W`. `[TREE]`
5. **A hand-over of `10 s` costs `0.024 Wh`** at the peak. A `60 s` hand-over costs `0.145 Wh`. Both fit inside the Buffer range of `0.3` to `1.5 Wh`. The Bare capacitor covers `2 s`. `[GENERAL]` from `host_margin.py`
